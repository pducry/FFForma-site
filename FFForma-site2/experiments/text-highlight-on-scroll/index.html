Every idea arrives shapeless. It hums. It insists. It refuses to explain itself. The work begins there — in the fog before the form.

We listen until the idea admits what it wants to become. Sometimes a brand. Sometimes a website. Sometimes a product, a series, an experiment that leads nowhere and teaches everything.

We draw, we build, we throw away. We let machines carry what is heavy so our hands stay free for what is delicate. Speed is not the point. Attention is.

A form is finished when nothing else can be removed — when function shows through the surface like light through paper.

Then we ship it, and the idea stops being ours. It belongs to whoever holds it. That is the whole craft: give the thing its shape, then let it go.
